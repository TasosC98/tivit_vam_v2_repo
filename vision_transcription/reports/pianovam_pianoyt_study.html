Where we stand

You have a video-only piano transcription model that is as good as the best published system on PianoVAM (V2N, ISMIR 2026), with 22 times fewer parameters. Trained on YouTube videos (PianoYT) from scratch, it reaches 63.9% at 100 ms — the level that S2S and V2R reach only after pre-training on another dataset, 4 points below PPAN. A PianoVAM model that has never seen PianoYT still collapses there, and more than a third of that gap is a timing offset in the PianoYT labels, which we can measure and correct.

The project in one paragraph

The input is a video of a piano keyboard filmed from above. The output is a MIDI file: for every note, which key (pitch), when the key goes down (onset) and when it comes up (offset). No audio is used. Later, every note will also get the hand that played it. Our model flattens the keyboard into a strip and gives each of the 88 keys its own column, so it never has to search for a key — it only has to decide, for a known place, “pressed or not”.

How our system works

The model is a visual version of Onsets and Frames (the classic audio transcription network): instead of a spectrogram it reads an image of the keyboard, and for every key and every video frame it answers two questions — “did a note start here?” (onset) and “is the key down?” (frame).

  1. Video framePianoVAM: 1920×1080, 60 fps. PianoYT: mostly 1920×1080, 24–60 fps. We decode at 720p and take 30 frames per second.
  2. Flatten the keyboardThe dataset gives the 4 keyboard corners. A perspective warp maps them to a straight strip — A0 at the left edge, C8 at the right edge.
  3. Keyboard strip1408 × 112 pixels, colour. 1408 / 88 = 16 px per key index.
  4. CNN (4 blocks)Each block halves width and height: 1408×112 → 88×7, with 192 channels. The same CNN runs on every frame.
  5. 88 key columns (the “tiles”)Average each of the 88 columns → one vector of 64 numbers per key per frame. This is ROI pooling with 88 fixed regions.
  6. Memory over time (BiGRU)One small bidirectional GRU reads each key’s vectors over the clip (32 frames ≈ 1.07 s), the same weights for all 88 keys.
  7. Two outputs per keyOnset probability and frame probability (the frame head also sees the onset). 88 × 2 numbers per frame.
  8. Decode → MIDIA note starts where onset ≥ threshold, and lasts while frame ≥ threshold. Thresholds are tuned on the valid split (PianoVAM: 0.90 / 0.70).

Tiles and ROIs in simple words

  • ROI (“region of interest”) just means “the part of the image we look at for one answer”. Here there are 88 ROIs, one per key, and they never move: the keyboard corners fix where each key is. Nothing has to be detected.
  • “Tile” is our name for one such column. The strip is cut into 88 equal columns of 16 px in pitch order. Real keys are not equally wide (white keys are wider than black keys), so a column is not the exact outline of its key — but each output column “sees” a wider area of the strip (the CNN’s receptive field), and it learns which pixels belong to its key.
  • Why it helps: pitch = position. The model never has to find a key, only to judge it. And because all 88 keys share the same weights, what it learns about one key helps all the others. This is why 1.3 M parameters are enough.
  • What it is not: not hand tracking and not moving boxes. Hand ROIs (from MediaPipe landmarks) are planned for E7, to say which hand played a note.

How we score (the evaluation protocol)

  1. Tune thresholds on valid only. The model is run once on every valid video; then many onset/frame threshold pairs are tried, and the best pair is kept (calibrate.py). The test set is never used for any choice.
  2. Score whole test videos with those thresholds, using mir_eval (the standard library all three papers use).
  3. Onset F1 at 50 and 100 ms: a predicted note is correct if it has the right pitch and its onset is within ±50 ms (or ±100 ms) of a true note. 50 ms is the audio standard and V2N’s main number; 100 ms is PPAN’s number for PianoYT. At 30 fps one frame is 33.3 ms.
  4. Onset + offset, two rules: mir_eval’s default (offset within 20% of the note length, at least 50 ms) and V2N’s stricter “++Off” (offset within the same ±50/±100 ms).
  5. Frame F1: every key × every time step is one yes/no. Our evaluator uses a 30 fps grid; the V2N comparison uses V2N’s 60 Hz grid.
  6. Average over recordings, each recording counts once. Differences between two systems are tested with a paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test over the recordings (two-sided; V2N used one-sided, which is less strict).

R8 · Timing — what the downloaded MIDI files show

New analysis for this report, computed on your PC with mir_eval from the predicted MIDI in out/ and your local label copies (PianoVAM TSVs, PianoYT MIDI). Sanity check: the 100 ms scores match the server’s to 4 decimals for every recording. For each matched note we take the error predicted onset − true onset (positive = we are late).

1 · PianoVAM: each recording has its own constant offset

  • How much it costs: removing each recording’s own offset would raise the mean onset F1 at 50 ms from 0.952 to 0.970 (02-14 20:10 alone: 0.928 → 0.985). One shift for all recordings together barely helps (best: all predictions 5 ms earlier → 0.953), because the offsets differ per recording. These are upper bounds computed with the test labels — a diagnosis, not a result.
  • Where it comes from: not the video files — your timestamp check shows every frame exactly on the i/fps grid, so our reader is right. It is the model and the decoding: black keys are detected about 12 ms later than white keys (R10), and the decoder takes the first frame above a high threshold (0.90), not the centre of the onset peak. V2N scores 0.965 on 02-14 20:10 with the same labels, which also points to our side, and the old model has different per-recording offsets, as expected if the decoding is involved.
  • The fix (new code): TIMING=1 tunes on valid the onset rule (first frame, or the sub-frame centre of the onset peak), one time shift for all keys, and an extra shift for black keys. Nothing is chosen on test, and no retraining is needed. It targets exactly the 50 ms score, where we tie with V2N (run E1t, Next steps).

2 · PianoYT: the labels are ~110 ms later than the key presses

The PianoVAM model learned onset = the moment the Disklavier registers the key. On all 20 PianoYT test videos its onsets come 60–235 ms before the PianoYT labels (typically 75–155 ms). The PianoYT labels come from audio, and YouTube video and sound are often not perfectly in sync. So part of the “zero-shot collapse” is a timing convention mismatch: measured on the same 19 videos against the model trained on PianoYT (0.482 at 100 ms), one shift closes 37% of the gap at 100 ms and 41% at 50 ms. A fair version (E3c): choose the shift on PianoYT valid, like the thresholds, and apply it to test.

3 · One PianoYT test video is clearly out of sync

This is the evidence for E6: PianoYT labels have a global delay plus per-video errors. V2N found the same kind of problem in R3 (MIDI–video offsets above 200 ms in 70 of 895 files).

“Pay Attention to the Keys” (PPAN)

Title
Pay Attention to the Keys: Visual Piano Transcription Using Transformers
Authors
Uros Zivanovic, Ivan Pilkov, Carlos Eduardo Cancino-Chacón
Venue
IJCAI 2025 (arXiv v1 13 Nov 2024, v2 4 Jul 2025)
Name
The paper calls its model “ViT”. V2N calls it PPAN; we do too.
Link
arxiv.org/abs/2411.09037

In one minute

  • They use a Vision Transformer (ViT-small) whose weights were pre-trained on video by other authors (“distilled ViT-small” of Wang et al., 2023).
  • Input: 6 grayscale frames at 30 fps (0.2 s), keyboard crop of 64 × 784 pixels.
  • They train on R3 (about 31 h, 2 professional pianists, real MIDI), then fine-tune on PianoYT using onsets only (“PianoYT ground truth offsets are of low quality”).
  • They score with a 100 ms onset tolerance (“∼±3 frames”), not 50 ms.
  • Their headline for us: PianoYT onset F-score 0.68, against 0.64 for S2S and V2R.

How they do it

BackboneViT-small: 12 layers, 384-dim embeddings, 6 heads; spatio-temporal “tubelet” patches
Pre-trainingVideo weights from Wang et al. (2023); the paper says results depend strongly on them
Input6 frames, 30 fps, grayscale, 64 × 784 crop; augmentation: noise, rotation, brightness/scale jitter
Outputs88 onset + 88 frame values per frame; offsets come from the frame output
LabelsOnset frame = 1, the frames before and after = 0.5
Training on R3AdamW (weight decay 0.05), cosine with 25% warm-up, 10 epochs, batch 384 on 4 GPUs, stochastic depth, class weights
Fine-tuning on PianoYTSGD (momentum 0.9), cosine with 10% warm-up, 5 epochs, batch 32 on 2 GPUs, onsets only
DecodingGaussian smoothing (onsets σ 0.2, frames σ 0.8), threshold 0.5
Scoringmir_eval; framewise, onsetwise, notewise F-score; onset tolerance 100 ms
PianoYT as they have ittrain 369,922 notes (17.2 h, 2 videos missing); test 33,535 notes (1.7 h); videos transcoded to 30 fps

What we do differently, and why

  1. Per-key tiles instead of a global transformer. Pitch is position, so we give each key its own column and share the weights. Why: a tiny model (1.3 M) that learns from little data and is easy to explain.
  2. Clean pre-training data. They pre-train on R3; we pre-train on PianoVAM, whose labels come from the piano itself. Why: E4 then asks a clear question — does exact-label pre-training help noisy YouTube data?
  3. We report 50 ms too. 100 ms hides timing errors (at 30 fps it is ±3 frames). Our timing analysis shows PianoYT labels carry a ~110 ms offset for an outside model — which a 100 ms tolerance partly absorbs.
  4. Thresholds tuned on valid instead of fixed, because the best threshold moves a lot between datasets (0.90 vs 0.20, Results R5).

What we can say — and what not yet

We can say

  • PPAN reports 0.68 onset F at 100 ms on PianoYT (R3 pre-training, onset-only fine-tuning).
  • Our E2, trained only on PianoYT, from scratch (1.3 M parameters), reaches 0.639 at 100 ms on our 20-video test set — the level of S2S and V2R (0.64), which needed R3 pre-training, and 0.04 below PPAN.
  • On PianoVAM, PPAN as retrained by V2N scores 84.9 / 94.1 (50 / 100 ms); we score 95.2 / 98.0 (V2N’s numbers for PPAN, not our run).

Not yet / not at all

  • “Better than PPAN on PianoYT” — not with E2 (0.639 < 0.68). E4 (pre-trained on PianoVAM) and E6 (sync-corrected labels) are the candidates.
  • Zero-shot: their ViT went R3 → PianoYT at 0.40; ours PianoVAM → PianoYT at 0.147 (0.266 with one timing shift). Different source datasets, so no direct claim. It does support their own remark that pre-trained weights drive transfer.
  • Exact same test set — 1 of 21 videos is missing for us.

In one minute

  • A dataset, not a model: 106 solo recordings, about 21 hours, 1,050,966 notes, 10 amateur pianists (70 recordings advanced, 26 intermediate, 10 beginner), practising on a Yamaha Disklavier — a piano that records every key as exact MIDI.
  • Every recording has: top-view video 1080p at 60 fps, audio (44.1 kHz, mono), MIDI, hand landmarks (MediaPipe Hands), semi-automatic fingering labels (about 95% precise on a checked sample), keyboard corner points, and metadata.
  • Audio, video and MIDI were aligned with a shared audio signal plus fine alignment by dynamic time warping (±2.5 s band).
  • Benchmarks in the paper: audio-only transcription and audio + video (video used as a filter). There is no video-only transcription experiment.

What we do differently, and why

  1. Video only. The dataset paper uses video only to help audio. We show video alone can reach onset scores in the same range as audio — which is the point of visual transcription (silent practice pianos, noisy rooms, old videos).

  • “Video matches audio.” That needs the audio model scored on the same 9 test recordings. It is a cheap, strong optional experiment (standard audio model, same split).
  • Any offset comparison — their offsets include the pedal.

V2N: the current state of the art

Title
Multi-Task Multi-Frame Visual Piano Transcription
Authors
Yonghyun Kim, Hoyeol Sohn, Juhan Nam, Alexander Lerch
Venue
Accepted to ISMIR 2026 (arXiv 4 Aug 2026)
Code
github.com/yonghyunk1m/V2N — includes predicted MIDI, which we used for the head-to-head test
Link
arxiv.org/abs/2608.03419

In one minute

  • V2N (“Video to Notes”) calls itself the first complete visual transcription system: onset, offset, key hold and velocity.
  • A ResNet-18 (grayscale, trained from scratch) reads 5-frame windows of an 800 × 144 keyboard image at 25 fps; three Conformer-style convolution blocks cover 1 second (25 frames); four BiLSTM heads give the four outputs.
  • 28.2 M parameters, 125 GFLOPs per 1-second clip.
  • They retrained four older systems (S2S, V2R, PPAN, and the video branch of Li et al.) on PianoVAM and R3 and beat them all.
  • They show that cross-dataset transfer collapses (PianoVAM ↔ R3: 0–6%) and blame the geometry: their warp fixes the pixel grid, not the keyboard inside it (keyboard ~784 px wide on R3 vs ~606 px on PianoVAM).

What we do differently, and why

  1. We fix the keyboard, not only the pixel grid. V2N names “geometry-invariant keyboard localization” as future work. Our warp maps the four keyboard corners to the full strip, so every key sits in the same 16-px column in every video — and our crop check confirms it on both datasets (median error 0.36 and 0.21 white keys).
  2. …and show that geometry is not enough. Our zero-shot still drops (14.7% at 100 ms). Our timing analysis explains part of it: a ~110 ms label delay on PianoYT (0.147 → 0.266 with one shift). The rest is appearance (pianos, light, cameras). V2N does not separate these causes; we can.
  3. Per-key tiles instead of a global feature. 1.3 M parameters instead of 28.2 M, with equal or better onset and offset scores on the same test.
  4. PianoYT. V2N does not use it. Clean vs noisy labels, fine-tuning and label sync are our territory.
  5. Sync as a topic. V2N found MIDI–video offsets above 200 ms in 70 of 895 R3 files (“all with MIDI preceding video”) and lists “joint sync alignment for noisy training data” as future work — that is our E6.

What we can say — and what not

We can say

  • On the same 9 test recordings, labels and scoring script, our 1.3 M model matches V2N at 50 ms (95.2 vs 94.7, p = 0.91) and beats it at 100 ms (98.0 vs 97.0, p = 0.008) and on onset + offset at 100 ms (97.1 vs 95.5, p = 0.004).
  • Our 98.0 at 100 ms is above every number in V2N’s table (best: Li et al. video branch, 97.4) — but we only tested significance against V2N.

We cannot say

  • “Better than V2N at 50 ms” — it is a tie.
  • “Better overall” — V2N also predicts velocity (82.8 / 84.5) and was tested on R3; we did neither.
  • Anything strong from n = 9 alone: say “on the 9 test recordings”, and add PianoYT and the ablations as extra evidence.

10 · The paper’s story, as it stands

  1. A 1.3 M-parameter per-key model matches or beats the state of the art (V2N, 28.2 M) on PianoVAM, on the same test and scoring.
  2. The training recipe matters as much as the architecture (84 → 95% with the same model).
  3. On PianoYT, trained from scratch, it reaches 0.639 at 100 ms — the level of R3-pre-trained S2S/V2R; E4 tests clean pre-training against PPAN’s 0.68.
  4. Cross-dataset: the warp solves per-key geometry, but that is not enough; more than a third of the zero-shot gap is label timing (~110 ms), the rest is appearance.
  5. Timing decides the 50 ms score: black keys are seen ~12 ms later; timing tuned on valid (E1t, E3c).
  6. Sync-corrected PianoYT labels (E6) and the audit document the dataset’s problems.
  7. Hands: PianoVAM’s skeleton tags are mirrored; with them fixed, notes + hands reach 97.7–100% hand accuracy on manual labels; E7 adds fingers and the hard cases.
